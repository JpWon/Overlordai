3 Your screen

You choose whether Overlord AI can see. On Windows there's no system prompt: screen capture stays off until you turn it on in the app's setup (or in Settings › Reconfigure permissions), and then the app looks at your whole main screen (monitor), not just the game window, so close anything private on it. On Linux, your operating system asks you to pick a screen to share; Overlord AI captures that whole screen, and your choice is remembered so you aren't asked at every launch. Capture runs only while a game is running: it starts when Overlord AI detects your game, never starts for a game it refuses as online multiplayer, and shuts off when it detects the game has closed. You can stop it at any time by turning screen capture off in Settings, from your system's screen-sharing controls on Linux, or by closing the app.

When frames are sent

  • When you ask. When you ask Overlord a question (F4, or your controller's prayer button), it looks at your game for the few seconds you're speaking, reads the text on screen, and sends what it saw with your question so it can answer. Because a controller's button can open the game's menu, it also remembers the last few seconds before you pressed: while a single-player game is running, it keeps about 8 seconds of reduced-size frames in your PC's memory (RAM), replacing them as it goes. With your question it sends up to four of those frames, the ones that differ, plus the text read from them. Nothing is kept after the answer plays.
  • Between questions, on your PC only. To keep up with where you are, Overlord reads the text on your screen (area names, on-screen labels) every few seconds and keeps the last 30 seconds of it in memory. That text stays on your PC unless it's sent along with a question, and it's never saved.
  • Automatically, during play. So its tips can keep up with where you are, Overlord also checks the screen on its own — but only while your game is the window in front, never when you've switched to another window. Apart from the moments around a prayer, it looks about once a second during in-game (NPC) conversations and while your character is standing still, and less often otherwise. A frame isn't sent if that part of the screen hasn't changed.

Where frames go

Frames go over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to our cloud service on Google Cloud, which checks your sign-in and passes the frame to Google Gemini to read it. The answer comes back to your PC. Our service doesn't save frames. Google handles the frames it receives under its own terms (section 9). To double-check an answer, our service may also send Anthropic's Claude that same question and the answer, with the text read from your screen and, on a second check, one reduced-size picture of your screen. Anthropic handles it under its own terms (section 9). DeepSeek never receives frames: when Gemini can't answer, DeepSeek gets only the text read from your screen and facts about the game.

Screen capture starts only when Overlord AI detects that a game is running, and it shuts off when it detects that the game has closed. Frames and the text read from your screen are held in your PC's short-term memory (RAM) only, are never written to disk, and are never stored by us. The few seconds kept for your next question stay in memory and are replaced as you play. Online multiplayer games never get this memory or the text reading. Overlord AI doesn't record video of your sessions, and it never stores what it saw.

Proactive tips

Overlord uses this on-screen context, together with what it reads from your game files (section 5), to offer tips on its own: cards on the HUD and, now and then, a short spoken nudge — for example, that you can craft something useful from what you're carrying, or a heads-up before a point of no return. Proactive tips are built on your PC from the same context; turning them off doesn't change what section 3 sends. You can turn proactive tips off in Settings, or say “quiet for now” to pause them, at any time.

4 Your voice and the co-pilot's voice

The microphone is only used after you press F4 to ask out loud, or the microphone button in Consult, and recording stops when you stop talking. The recording goes through our cloud service to Google Gemini, which turns it into text, and the text is used to answer you. Our service doesn't save recordings. On your PC, the recording is kept in a single temporary file that your next question overwrites.

The co-pilot's voice is made on your PC by a voice model that ships with the app, so the text of spoken answers stays on your PC. Google Cloud Text-to-Speech is only the fallback: on a PC that is too slow for the voice model, or if the voice on your PC fails, the text goes through our service to Google and the audio comes back. The startup and goodbye lines are recordings that ship with the app. Your personal greeting ("Welcome back, …") uses the display name from your account; it's made once and then saved on your PC.

5 What stays on your PC

  • Key presses. The app listens for the F4 key, and while a combo card is open it notices which buttons you press so it can tick off each step. Key presses never leave your PC. The app never sends key presses or mouse movements to a game.
  • Your game and your progress. To know which game you're playing, Overlord AI reads the install records of Steam, GOG Galaxy and the Epic Games Launcher. To guide you, it reads your save files, your game's quest and progress files, its log files, some of its settings files (for example, your button layout), and the game's own data files on your PC — to work out your current mission, where you are, your inventory and what you've won, and to name your items and what you can craft. It only reads them: it never changes a save or a game file, never reads the game's memory, and neither your saves nor the game's files ever leave your PC. You can switch game-file reading off at any time in Settings › Reconfigure permissions.
  • Steam achievements. For a Steam game, Overlord AI reads the achievement files Steam keeps on your PC (which achievements you've unlocked, and when) to note which bosses you've beaten and which places you've reached. It only reads them and never changes them, and nothing from them leaves your PC: they aren't part of what is sent with a question or kept on your account.
  • What is sent with a question. When you ask a question, or Overlord offers a tip, it sends a short summary so the answer fits where you are: the game; your level and stats; your current mission and the step you're on in it, including the quest progress your save records for that mission; your inventory summary; the last place Overlord saw you; your button layout and the language you play in; your plan for the next steps and any items you're collecting for it; what you told Overlord you want tonight (for example "no spoilers" or "about an hour"); and whether its last few tips at this game worked. It goes with the question to our cloud service and the AI that writes the answer, and isn't kept there afterwards. Nothing else from your saves is sent, and no account details or file names go with it.
  • What is kept. While you're signed in, we keep one progress summary per game on your account, so Overlord can pick up where you left off on any PC: the game, your level, your location, your current mission, the missions you've completed, and a summary of your inventory. Your stats go with a question but are not kept. Each newer summary replaces the last; we keep no history. It is deleted when you delete your account. Signed out, nothing is kept.
  • What never happens. Overlord AI never edits a save, never uploads a raw save file, never uploads the game's data files or anything built from them (only your own item names go in your summary), and never reads or changes your game's memory.
  • Game memory. The app never reads or changes a game's memory, and never injects code into a game.
  • The game's sound. So it never talks over a game's characters, Overlord listens to the sound your game itself makes, on your PC, for one thing only: whether someone in the game is speaking right now. It hears only the game's own audio, not your microphone or other apps. Nothing is recorded, stored or sent.
  • The Scout. For a game Overlord doesn't know well yet, it looks at the names of the files in the game's install folder and in the usual folders where games keep saves and settings, to learn which engine the game uses and where it keeps its saves and settings. It reads a save only to identify its format, never changes anything, and never looks anywhere else.
  • Why it said that. For each answer and tip, Overlord keeps a short note on your PC of what it used and why it spoke when it did (you can see it with Settings › Show why). The notes stay on your PC.
  • Settings and progress you make in the app, and a cache of guides and your personal greeting.

6 Your account, licence and usage

We keep these in Supabase, linked to your account:

  • Account: your email address, display name and password (stored by Supabase as a hash). If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive the name, email and profile photo they share.
  • Licence and trial: your plan, licence key, how much trial time you've used, and a device identifier: a one-way scrambled (hashed) version of your computer's machine ID, with the device name and operating system. This is how the trial is one per person and how plans count PCs.
  • Usage: how many answers, voice lines and research requests your account used each day, and their estimated cost, so we can apply fair-use limits.
  • Progress: your game sessions (which game, when they started and ended, how long), milestones such as bosses beaten, loot found, areas reached, in-game conversations won and the assists Overlord gave, and whether a tip card was used. These power the stats on your dashboard.
  • Game research: when you play a game we haven't mapped, the game's name and area names are researched from public sources with Google's Gemini and Google Search. The resulting guide is saved in our Google Cloud storage and linked to your account. It holds facts about the game, not about you.
  • Which games people want: when you play a game we haven't mapped, the app sends, once per play session, the game's Steam ID and name, a guess at its engine, how long you played and how much the Scout found. It is stored against a one-way code made from your account and that game, so we can count players without knowing who they are; the code can't be turned back into your account.
  • Game facts the Scout learns: the general facts it found (the engine, and the pattern of where the game keeps its saves and settings, for example %USERPROFILE%\Saved Games\<Game>, with your user name, Steam account and drive removed) are shared so the next player starts with them. Never your files, their contents, your names or your account. They are stored with the same kind of one-way, per-game code, and a fact is only trusted once several players report the same thing.
  • Install count: the first time Overlord starts after a new install, it makes a random install number and sends it once, with the app version and whether you're on Windows or Linux, so we can count how many people installed Overlord. Updates never send a new one. It isn't linked to your account, your computer's ID or your files.
  • Founder Access and the waitlist: if you claim Founder Access we store your email, the plan you chose, any feedback you write, how you signed in, a referral code if you came from a partner link, and your browser type. Waitlist sign-ups store your email.
  • Error reports: while Send error reports is on (Settings; on unless you turn it off), the installed app sends a short report when something fails (for example the microphone, an update, or the app itself), after an update, the first time you install, sign in and ask a question, and after each question you ask. A report holds only: what kind of problem it was and where in the app, how often and when it happened, the app version, your operating system and its version, how long each step of an answer took and which steps were used (for example whether the answer was double-checked), an anonymous install code (a one-way scrambled code made on your PC, not your licence device ID), and, when a game ends, the names and ages of any of our own helper programs still running. It never holds what was on your screen, what you said or typed, your saves or game files, your name, email or computer name; folder paths and email addresses are removed before anything is sent. Reports are linked to your account, kept for 90 days and then deleted, and used only to find and fix problems. An automated nightly review, run with Anthropic's Claude, groups the day's reports to find bugs; reports from our own team's accounts and computers are left out of it. You can turn reports off at any time; nothing already sent is resent.
  • Support: emails you send us.

8 The website

The website is hosted by Vercel. When you visit, these tools can run:

  • Vercel Web Analytics counts page views and the pages people visit, without cookies.
  • Google Ads tag (gtag.js) measures whether our Google ads lead to visits and sign-ups. It sets cookies and sends Google information about your visit.
  • Reddit Ads pixel measures whether our Reddit ads lead to visits and sign-ups, and lets us show our ads on Reddit to people who visited. It sets cookies and sends Reddit information about your visit.
  • Rewardful runs our partner (affiliate) program. If you arrive through a partner's link, it stores a referral code in a cookie so the partner can be credited.
  • Sign-in on the website uses Supabase, which keeps your session in your browser's storage. Your light or dark theme and your cookie choice are saved there too.

Your choice

If you visit from the EU, EEA, UK or Switzerland, the Google Ads tag, the Reddit pixel and Rewardful don't load until you press Accept on our cookie banner. Everywhere else they load by default, and you can turn them off. Anyone can change their choice at any time with Cookie settings at the bottom of every page. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a Reject. The site works the same either way.

The app's installers are downloaded from GitHub Releases, which logs downloads under GitHub's own policy.

11 How we use it

  • To provide Overlord AI: answer your questions, show tip cards, speak, research games, run your account and plan (needed to perform our contract with you).
  • To keep it fair and safe: one trial per person, device limits, fair-use limits, and preventing abuse (our legitimate interests).
  • To improve it: deciding which games to map next and fixing what goes wrong (our legitimate interests).
  • To talk to you: receipts, account notices and, with your permission, news about Overlord AI.
  • To measure our ads and partner program on the website.

We don't sell your personal information, and we don't use your screen frames or recordings to build a profile of you. The Google Ads tag and the Reddit pixel on our website measure our ads, and the Reddit pixel lets us show our ads to past visitors; under some US state laws that can count as "sharing" for advertising. You can opt out with Cookie settings at the bottom of any page, or with a Global Privacy Control signal (section 8).

12 Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to show you, correct or delete the personal data we hold about you, or to delete your account.

  • EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland (GDPR): you also have the rights to restrict or object to processing, to data portability, and to withdraw consent at any time. You can complain to your local data protection authority.
  • California and other US states: you can ask what we collect and why, ask us to delete or correct it, and opt out of the sale or sharing of your information. We don't sell it. We won't treat you differently for using these rights.

Email privacy@overlordai.co from the address on your account. We may need to confirm it's you. We answer within one month (45 days for requests under US state laws).

13 International transfers

We're based in the United States, and our service providers process data in the United States and other countries. DeepSeek processes the text it receives in China. Where a provider offers standard contractual clauses or a similar safeguard for international transfers, we rely on it. By using the app's screen and voice features you understand that frames and recordings are processed in these countries.

14 Security

Everything the app sends travels over HTTPS. The app never holds AI provider keys: our cloud service adds them, after checking your sign-in. Your licence key is encrypted with your operating system's secure storage. No system is perfectly secure, and we'll tell you without undue delay if a breach affects your data.

15 Children

Overlord AI isn't for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal data, write to privacy@overlordai.co and we'll delete it.

16 Changes

When what the app collects or sends changes, we update this policy in the same release and change the date at the top. For a material change, we'll email account holders before it takes effect.
